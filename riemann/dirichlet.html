Why only Dirichlet

The proof claims the Riemann Hypothesis for the zeta function and for every Dirichlet L-function, and nothing more. As we read it, that is not caution: the method stops where the free Frobenius stops.

The architecture of a proof   The map   Stepanov and the new proof

Why it matters

Dirichlet L-functions belong to abelian extensions of the rational numbers: the primes are sorted by their class in a finite abelian group, and each character of that group has its own L-function. A proof about abelian objects that uses abelian structure is using the structure of the problem, not leaning on a crutch. The question worth asking is the next one: which parts of the argument would survive one step beyond, and which would not.

The natural step beyond is GL(2): the L-function of an elliptic curve, or of a modular form, without complex multiplication. Below we take the pieces of the map one at a time and ask what happens to each there.

Abelian structure, used twice

  1. Sorting makes the weights whole. Divisibility can only count with whole, non-negative weights. The sum over primes can be assembled from such weights because the primes are sorted into the classes of a finite abelian group: inside each class the count is non-negative, and the complex characters are recovered from the classes by orthogonality. See the first row of the dictionary.
  2. One field serves every place. A single CM field gives a local Frobenius at every inert prime, because a curve with complex multiplication by it is supersingular at exactly those primes, and the same field supplies a period with the constant the main term needs. See a Frobenius at every inert prime and the right size.

The two facts behind the odd powers in the weight and behind the height-two formal group are the same fact: at an inert prime the residue field has p² elements.

What would extend, and what would not

Both uses of abelian structure fail for GL(2) without complex multiplication, while the machinery around them does not depend on it. The plausible extensions are therefore the ones that keep a CM field: Hecke L-functions of CM fields, modular forms with complex multiplication, perhaps Artin L-functions with finite image. This fits the announcement, which claims Dirichlet L-functions and stops there. We have not tried any extension and do not claim that one works.

The author's own view points the same way (post of 24 November 2026): over number fields there is no known object of the kind written H¹ on which a single Frobenius acts, and he reads any real difficulty as a sign that the present setup is clumsy. In our terms, the free Frobenius that a CM field supplies to abelian problems is exactly what such an object would supply in general. That gloss is ours, not his.
