From primes to zeros

The top row of the map: how the proof turns a sum over primes into a count of divisibility, what bound it proves on that sum, and how the bound becomes the absence of zeros.

The quantity the proof controls

Let Λ(n) be von Mangoldt's function: ln p when n is a power of a prime p, and 0 otherwise. The archive works with a smoothed sum of it (Formalism.Gz):

G(x) = ∑n ≤ x Λ(n) (x/n − 1) − x ln x + (1 + γ) x

The weight x/n − 1 is a smoothing of the plain count ψ(x) = ∑n ≤ x Λ(n): the sum equals x times the integral of ψ(t)/t2 from 1 to x. Its main term is x ln x − (1 + γ) x, so G(x) is the error. For a Dirichlet character χ the archive uses the same sum with Λ(n)χ(n), whose main term is −x·L′/L(1, χ) (Deduction.Gchi).

By Riemann's explicit formula, each zero ρ of ζ contributes to G(x) a term of size about xRe ρ / |ρ|2. So G(x) ≤ x1/2 + ε for all large x is exactly the kind of statement that rules out zeros to the right of 1/2 + ε. The rest of this page is how the proof gets there and how it draws the conclusion.

Step 1: a sum over primes as a count of divisibility

Legendre's formula says how many times a prime p divides a!:

vp(a!) = ⌊a/p⌋ + ⌊a/p2⌋ + ⌊a/p3⌋ + …

Multiply by ln p and add over all p, and you get ln(a!) written as a sum over prime powers: a count of divisibility is a sum of Λ. The archive uses a variant of this count at each prime p that is inert in the CM field K (SizeData₂.vonMangoldtClassSum):

∑i odd max(0, a/pi − w)

with the floor replaced by a ramp, a threshold w subtracted, and only odd powers counted. The odd powers at inert primes are exactly where the weight 1 − χK lives: χK(pi) = (−1)i when p is inert, so 1 − χK(pi) is 2 for odd i and 0 for even i, and it is 0 at every split prime. Summed with the factor 2·ln p, as the archive does, the count is the smoothed prime sum with weight 1 − χK(n), apart from the ramified primes, which the code treats separately.

The proof then sorts this count by characters. Deduction.ch_balance is an exact identity, not an inequality: expanding the count over all characters of a finite abelian group attached to the field (E0 × H in the code), by orthogonality of characters, gives

ASK − MAIN = De

where ASK is the count, the trivial character contributes the main term a·ln a − (1 + γ)·a of the same shape as in G(x), and De collects the twisted prime sums of the other characters. Because the count is a count of divisibility, it can be compared with how divisible an actual object is. That comparison is the rest of the map: the ladder makes the objects divisible, the size is known exactly, and the prohibition says divisibility cannot exceed size. What comes out is ASK ≤ MAIN plus errors, and the balance makes the errors of size √x.

The residual on windows

The output of all that is UpperTheory.Comparison₂.core_gm: for a "level" Λ and every ε > 0, for all large x, if the hypotheses packaged in EAtH and CoreAtH hold, then

Gm(x) ≤ x1/2 + (eD + eL + 7)ε

Here Gm (Formalism.HC.Level.Gm) is G(x) plus a weighted sum of the character versions for the characters of the level, each weight positive. The constants eD and eL are fixed; since ε is arbitrary, the exponent is 1/2 plus anything.

Two things about this bound matter for the next step. It is one-sided: only an upper bound on Gm is proved. And it is only needed on windows, stretches [Y, Y1+δ] that start arbitrarily far out, not for all large x. According to the trace in ishaan2006 (mechanism/TRACE.md, sections 23 and 24), which we have not checked line by line, the windows are where the rest of the argument can deliver the bound, and the characters other than the target get their bounds from a zero-density estimate, by choosing auxiliary moduli away from the few bad ones.

Landau's lemma, window form

In 1905 Landau proved that a Dirichlet series with nonnegative coefficients has a singularity on the real axis at the point where it stops converging. Applied to prime sums it says: if a combination of prime sums with nonnegative coefficients grows at most like xθ, then the corresponding function has no poles to the right of θ. The poles come from the zeros of the L-functions, so a one-sided growth bound rules out zeros. Positivity is what makes one side enough.

The archive proves a version that needs the bound only on windows (Deduction.zeroFree_of_rpow_window, through Deduction.lemmaL). For a CharFamily, a finite list of Dirichlet characters χi with positive weights ai:

If for every Y0 there is Y ≥ Y0 with Gm(y) ≤ C·yθ for every y in [Y, Y1+δ], where 0 < θ < 1, then no L(s, χi) vanishes for Re s > θ.

Read the other way round, this is the card on the map: a zero to the right of θ would push Gm above C·yθ somewhere in every window far enough out.

The positivity is built into the definitions. The weights of a CharFamily are positive. The set of points the argument has to exclude (Deduction.zeroSet) contains each zero together with its complex conjugate. And the record Deduction.PoleData requires the transform to have a simple pole at each such point, with a positive real residue at the real ones. The proof (Deduction.landau_window) works with the Laplace transform of Gm in the variable u = ln y and compares high derivatives of it at a real point and at points shifted along the imaginary axis; a pole to the right of θ makes these derivatives too large for the window bound (landau_window_key, landau_window_contra). We read this proof at the level of its statements. The classical theorem in the archive (Formalism.HC.Landau.zero_free_of_Chebyshev_growth), which needs the bound for all large x, is not used.

The top of the proof

Applied to the families the proof builds, the lemma gives UpperTheory.FinalTheorems.everyCharacter: no Dirichlet L-function has a zero with real part above 1/2, apart from the pole at s = 1. From there Formalism.VW.rh_of_every reaches the Riemann Hypothesis by the classical symmetry described in what the hypothesis says. Why the method stops at Dirichlet L-functions is on its own page.
