The family exists

The last support, and the simplest: the objects the proof needs can actually be found.

What it says

The proof works with a family of data, one for each small ε; in our reading these are the auxiliary primes and the objects built from them. The family is defined by a filter, a list of requirements the data must meet. That the filter lets something through follows from standard theorems of number theory: Chebotarev's density theorem, Poitou–Tate duality, Minkowski's bound on finite subgroups of GLn(Q). It also uses what is known about zeros without any hypothesis: the classical zero-free regions and zero-density estimates.

There is no circle here. The facts about zeros used to build the family are ones proved long ago, unconditionally; the Riemann Hypothesis is not assumed anywhere along the way. As we read it, the existence of the family has no hard step.

One point is more than assembly. The auxiliary primes must be chosen so that the twisted prime sums of their characters are small, of size x1/2 + ε. By Jaswal's guide, the archive shows this not for every pair but on average, by a mean square over the moduli and Markov's inequality, so that most pairs are good. This is one of the two places where the exponent 1/2 enters; the other is the balance.
