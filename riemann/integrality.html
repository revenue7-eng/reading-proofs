Whole numbers on every rung

The ladder only works if every rung stays inside the whole numbers and its size is known exactly. This is the support that guarantees both.

What the ladder needs

Every argument in the proof rests on one fact: a nonzero whole number is at least 1. Divisibility means something only for whole numbers, and the prohibition applies only to lattices, the many-dimensional whole numbers. So the pairing between neighbouring rungs of the ladder must be integral.

And the comparison of divisibility with size is meant to detect an error of size about √x against quantities of size about x. A size known only roughly would hide exactly what is being measured. So the norms on every rung must be known exactly.

How the archive gets both

It builds a tower of theta lifts and uses Rallis's inner product formula. The formula says that on each floor of the tower the norm is multiplied by a value of an L-function at a whole-number point. So the size of every rung is an explicit product, known exactly, and the lifts carry whole-number lattices to whole-number lattices.

The formula itself is not quoted. The archive derives it by the doubling method of Piatetski-Shapiro and Rallis, from the Siegel–Weil formula and the analytic continuation of Eisenstein series.

What we expected, and what we found

When we began reading, we thought the new idea of the proof might hide here, in the formula for the sizes of theta lifts. It does not. This part re-derives a known method, carefully and in full, inside the archive. It is a large body of work, and the ladder could not stand without it, but as we read it, it contains no new idea.

What does matter, and what the integer page describes, is the price of keeping each rung integral prime by prime. That price decides whether the exponent comes out as 1/2 or 2/3, and it is part of the idea.
