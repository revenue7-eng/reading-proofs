The right size

Divisibility only bounds something if the size of the object is known independently and exactly. Here is how the proof measures it.

The architecture of a proof   The map   A Frobenius at every inert prime

Why the factorial is not enough

The proof runs like Chebyshev's argument with a better whole number. An object divisible by many primes, many times, cannot be small. Comparing its divisibility with its size bounds the error in the prime count.

For that comparison to say anything, the size must be known by a different route from the divisibility, and it must carry the same constants as the prime count: Euler's constant γ and the number L′/L(1, χK) from the field. The factorial fails on both counts. Its size and its divisibility are given by the same formula of Legendre, and it misses the term γ·a. We checked this by computer while reading.

How the proof measures size

The objects of the proof live in whole-number lattices of polynomials, the Fock model of the Weil representation. A monomial of degree A there has norm

A! · ΩA

where Ω is a period of the field with complex multiplication. The factorial is still there, but it now comes with ΩA, and that factor brings the missing constants. Taking logarithms, the term A·ln Ω is linear in A, exactly where the factorial fell short.

What the period knows

A classical theorem of Chowla and Selberg says that a period with complex multiplication is a product of values of the Gamma function. Gamma knows Euler's constant: its logarithmic derivative at 1 is −γ. Through the functional equation of L(s, χK), ln Ω turns into a combination of γ and L′/L(1, χK). In the archive this is proved from Kronecker's limit formula.

For the simplest case, the field Q(i) and Gauss's lemniscate, the identity reads

ln Ω = ½ (γ − L′/L(1, χ)) + ln π − ½ ln 2

where χ is the character mod 4 and Ω = 2.622… is the lemniscate constant. We worked out this form ourselves from Lerch's formula and the functional equation, as an illustration; the proof uses a larger field, and its formula has the same shape. The check below computes Ω three independent ways.

The first line is Gauss's arithmetic–geometric mean, the second Chowla–Selberg, the third the prime-count constants: γ, and L′/L(1, χ) summed term by term as −Σ χ(n) ln n / n divided by π/4. Push the slider and the third line closes in on the other two. γ itself is entered as a known constant.

Size and divisibility agree

With the period in place, ln(A! · ΩA) reproduces the main term of the prime count, constants included, and agrees with the divisibility through the term linear in A. What is left over is the error, and that is what the rest of the proof bounds.

The same field that supplies the period also supplies the local Frobenius and sorts the primes. That is why the constants match: they are not fitted, they come from one object.
